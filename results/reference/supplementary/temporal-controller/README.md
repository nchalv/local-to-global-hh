# Temporal-Controller Policy Ablation

## Purpose and configuration

This controlled experiment isolates the policy used to release Space-Saving
capacity after the current deployment has accumulated sufficient evidence. It
uses `m=100`, `n=200`, an approximately 60,000-item window, a 15,000-key
universe, `epsilon_M=0.15`, and `alpha=0.95`. Ambiguity adjustment is disabled.
All four policies share the same upward-sizing rule and differ only in their
choice and validation of lower-capacity probes:

- **Bracket probing** searches between previously insufficient and sufficient
  capacities.
- **Guarded bracket probing** retains demand exposed by a failed probe while
  selecting from the same bracket.
- **Pressure-gated comfort probing** permits a reduction only when the observed
  pressure ratio is at most 0.5.
- **Guarded margin comfort**, the selected policy, uses observed margin
  headroom to choose a bounded reduction and retains failed-probe demand during
  recovery.

The workloads alternate between an easier profile with eight clearly heavy
keys and a difficult profile with 65 keys close to the threshold. Their global
composition and placement change as a sharp step, smooth ramp, three short
bursts, or repeated block oscillation. The shaded regions mark controlled
stress phases. The intermediate and high-stress placements were constructed by
the retained MILP generator solely to create repeatable changes in local sketch
difficulty. They are not baselines or placement policies used in the paper's
final comparison, which instead uses round robin and visibility suppression.

## How the figures were produced

The `csv/` directory contains all 16 per-window evaluator outputs: four
workloads times four policies. `summary.csv` is recomputed with
`summarize_temporal_grid.py`; the four figure pairs are then generated by
`plot_temporal_grid.py`. Exact plot-only commands are given in
`docs/SUPPLEMENTARY_RESULTS.md`. A complete evaluator rerun is available as:

```bash
./artifact temporal-controller
```

The aggregate metrics are means across completed windows. Threshold-normalized
error is `n * AAE / N`. A service violation means that the observed uncertainty
exceeded the configured margin target. It is not a missed-heavy-hitter event:
all 16 runs retain heavy-hitter recall 1.000.

## Reading the figures

**`temporal_controller_ablation_scheduled`.** The four bar-chart panels compare
threshold-normalized error, service-violation rate, mean deployed capacity
`q/n`, and `(q/n) * normalized error`. This is the compact policy comparison.

**`temporal_controller_capacity_accuracy_curve_scheduled`.** Each point is one
policy's mean capacity and normalized error on a workload. It exposes the
tradeoff hidden by a single aggregate score.

**`temporal_controller_stepwise_capacity_scheduled`.** Rows are workloads and
columns are policies. Lines show deployed `q/n`; triangles mark downward
probes, crosses failed probes, and red points service violations. This figure
shows response speed, recovery, and whether capacity is eventually released.

**`temporal_controller_stepwise_error_scheduled`.** The same grid shows
per-window normalized error. The dashed horizontal line is the configured
`epsilon_M=0.15` target, while the shading locates the scheduled transitions.

## Takeaways by workload

| Workload | Main observation |
|---|---|
| Sharp step | Pressure gating uses the least capacity (`1.24n`) and has the fewest service violations, at the highest mean error. The selected policy stays near the middle of this tradeoff. |
| Smooth ramp | Bracket probing obtains the lowest error and violation rate by retaining the most capacity (`1.56n`). Comfort policies release more state and accept moderately higher error. |
| Short bursts | Guarded bracket gives the lowest error but uses the most capacity. The selected policy nearly matches its error, ties the lowest violation rate, and uses less state. |
| Block oscillation | The selected policy achieves the lowest error by retaining more capacity through repeated changes. Pressure gating minimizes violations but has higher error. |

No policy dominates every workload and metric. Guarded margin comfort has the
lowest capacity-error product on the sharp-step and oscillating workloads and is
within 0.5% of the guarded-bracket result on short bursts. Bracket probing leads
on the smooth ramp by retaining more capacity. The experiment therefore
supports the selected controller as a balanced default that releases memory,
reacts to recurring difficulty, and preserves recall, rather than as an optimal
policy for every temporal pattern.
